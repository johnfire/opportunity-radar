## What a good first pilot looks like
- Small enough that one decision-maker can say yes (roughly 20–250 people; to be checked).
- A process with handoffs and queues (invoice approval, order intake, onboarding, case files), which is where the
  method finds "17 minutes of work in 22 days".
- Not in financial distress (no budget, no time) and not a giant (no procurement office, no works council fights).
- Willing to be a reference or case study, in writing.
- Reachable, ideally by a warm introduction.

## How to approach (general knowledge; confirm before sending anything)
- Prefer a warm introduction (IHK Schwaben contacts, SoftB referrals, per your Open Brain notes) over a cold message.
- In Germany, unsolicited commercial email or phone contact, even to businesses, is restricted under the UWG; a letter,
  an introduction or a reply to something they published is safer. Ask a lawyer or the IHK if unsure.
- Offer a specific, bounded thing: one process, 2–4 interviews, about two weeks, a written case study in return,
  clear data-handling terms (including who is interviewed and consent).

## Pilot terms to settle before the first conversation
1. What the client pays (nothing, a symbol price, or a discounted fixed fee) and what you get in return in writing.
2. Interviewee consent, and the works council if the firm has one.
3. Which model providers are allowed for this client (the workbench's "sensitive" label) and a data-processing agreement.
4. How the savings estimate will be checked afterward.
